Cleara is
selling trust.

The AI talent agent has credible founders, real marketplace traction signals, and a strong launch. But the central question is not whether Cleara can create introductions. It is whether its acquisition and data practices support the candidate-first position it claims.

The trust gap

02 / THE CORE RISK

Cleara’s stated promise is candidate advocacy: understand a person, match them to the right startup, and make a warm introduction. The complaints describe a different first touch—a simulated recruiter relationship that begins before clear permission.

Complaint pattern, not a single anecdote

5-star reviews~77%
1-star reviews~20%

Trustpilot is approximately 4.0/5 across 76 reviews, but the distribution is bimodal. The report treats both positive and negative reviews as unverified user opinions; the negative cluster earns attention because the complaints are specific, repeated, and independently corroborated.

What reviewers repeatedly describe

  • Human-sounding AI personas “Sarah,” “Sophia,” “Lisa,” and “Daniel” present in the first person.
  • Unrelated sender domain Messages from getblona.com, connected to founder Sebastian Scott’s prior company Blona.
  • Unclear consent LinkedIn information allegedly used to create profiles, with some users auto-enrolled after sharing a resume for a specific role.
  • Persistence Multiple follow-ups, including to inboxes recipients say they do not use professionally.
Important qualification: These are allegations and user reports, not adjudicated findings. Cleara replies to many negative reviews, denies posting fake jobs, says some listings are anonymized for confidentiality, and offers to resolve complaints. That responsive posture does not remove the need to clarify sourcing and consent.

Decision guide

05 / WHAT TO DO

If you’re a candidate

The upside is real for some users: fast introductions and at least some reported hires. The first interaction is the risk surface.

Do this: Before sending a resume, ask for the named role, company, data source, and exact opt-in. Request deletion if you never consented. EU users can consider GDPR rights; California users can consider CCPA rights.

If you’re an employer

The success-fee structure limits initial downside, and one independent Product Hunt reviewer reported two engineering hires with strong profile quality.

Do this: Run a controlled pilot and benchmark candidate quality, consent language, and candidate experience against Standout, Jack & Jill, and Dex.

If you’re an investor

Do not underwrite the headline growth numbers alone. The next value inflection is trust durability, not another candidate-count milestone.

Watch: audited retained revenue; complaint mix over time; data-sourcing disclosures; any GDPR, CIPA, or UCL escalation; and differentiation against better-funded rivals.

The diligence question to ask Cleara

“When did this person opt in, what exactly did they opt in to, and can you show us the disclosure they saw?”
